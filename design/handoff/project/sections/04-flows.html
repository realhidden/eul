Key UX flows

Wireframe fidelity by design — the pixels for the bar and panel are in §2. Each flow is written as the steps a user actually takes.

4.1 First run — value in under 30 seconds

Open eul.

No onboarding window, no permission dialogs — the core monitors need none. The anchor and the default strip (CPU, NET) appear in the bar within one refresh tick.

One coach mark.

A small popover anchored to the glyph: "eul lives here. Click for detail." with a secondary "Choose what to show…" link into Menu Bar settings. Dismisses on any interaction; never shown again.

First click.

The panel opens; everything is visible without configuration. Login-item enrollment is offered here as a single line at the panel's first-run footer ("Start eul at login — Enable"), not as a dialog.

Direct-download and App Store first runs are identical; the App Store build simply has fewer monitors everywhere (no temps/fans), with zero ceremony about it.

4.2 The glance — what 500 ms delivers

Peripheral pass.

Glyph monochrome → all normal; nothing else needs reading. This is the whole product for 95% of glances.

Chosen numbers.

If pinned, CPU and NET read as stable-width figures — no flapping, no color, no motion to catch the eye when nothing changed.

Exception.

Amber or red eye → the glance has a verb: click. The bar itself never explains; explanation is the panel's job.

4.3 The investigation — "why is my fan spinning?"

Click the anchor (interaction 1).

The panel opens already answering: the header reads the verdict ("CPU elevated — Xcode"), the CPU tile is the only colored object, auto-expanded, with per-core load and the culprit process pinned with its icon and core-equivalent figure.

Optionally, go one deeper (interaction 2).

Switch the process lens (CPU / Memory / Network) or expand another tile. The 10-minute sparkline answers "did this just start?" without any further navigation.

Two interactions is the budget (P1); the common case resolves in one. Quitting the runaway process stays Activity Monitor's job in v2.0 — eul diagnoses, it does not operate.

Wireframe-only by agreement. Deleted from 1.x: component ordering, font picker, per-monitor refresh, icon toggles. The philosophy: settings choose among good states (P5).

4.5 The abnormal moment — signaling without interrupting

Threshold trips (sustained).

The anchor's right eye fills amber on the next refresh tick. No animation, no sound, no notification, no badge. If pinned, the related slot does not change — color stays unique to the anchor so it reads even at anchor-only width.

User clicks when ready.

The panel opens pre-sorted on the cause (§4.3). If the user never clicks, eul never escalates: critical (red) is reserved for OS-reported heavy thermal/memory pressure or a nearly-dead boot disk — states where acting matters.

Condition clears.

Hysteresis holds the state until the signal stays below threshold for 2× its window, then the eye quietly empties. No "all clear" celebration.

Interruption policy, complete: eul may post exactly one kind of notification (it became unreachable, §2.5), may never steal focus, may never animate to attract attention, and may use color only via the health engine. This policy is a principle (P3), not a preference.

4.6 Enabling fan control — the P6 ceremony

Live in the panel prototype (Exhibit C: FANS tile → “Control fans…”). The binding constraint: the privileged-helper request happens only at the moment the user first enables control — never at launch, never during onboarding, never as a badge or banner.

Discovery, without nagging.

The FANS tile always shows readings. Its expanded view contains one quiet affordance: “Control fans…”. Users who want control find it exactly where the temperatures that motivate it live; everyone else never learns the feature exists.

The explainer.

One sheet, four short lines: what installs (a small helper that talks to the SMC), why admin approval is required (writing to hardware), that targets are clamped and macOS can always cool past them, and how to remove it. Buttons: “Not now” / “Install Helper…”. No urgency, no preselection.

The handoff.

macOS takes over (System Settings → Login Items & Extensions). The sheet waits, stating where the approval is happening. Approval unlocks the controls in place — no restart, no second dialog.

Denial is a non-event.

The sheet closes back to readings. The affordance remains, unchanged; a later attempt replays the same explainer. eul never re-asks on its own, never counts attempts, never nags.

Using it.

Per-fan Auto / Manual / Boost. Manual exposes a slider clamped to the hardware’s real min/max and reports target vs. actual — divergence is the safety model made visible (the system is cooling past you). The safety strip (“Clamped to hardware limits · macOS can always cool past your setting · Reverts to Auto when eul quits”) is permanently attached to the controls — three capsules, never a dialog, never dismissible.

Removal is first-class.

“Remove helper…” lives in the same expanded tile where enabling lived (mirrored by a Settings row). One click uninstalls the daemon, reverts fans to Auto, and confirms in plain language. Removing root components must be easier than installing them — that asymmetry is the trust message.

4.7 Personalization — user stories, and the line between taste and assembly

1.x conflated two different needs. Assembly — reordering text components, picking fonts, per-monitor refresh rates — is deleted (P5): that was the user building the product. Taste — which units the numbers speak, which monitors matter to this person, when eul speaks up — is legitimate and now designed properly. The fence, quotably: you choose what eul shows, which units it speaks, and when it speaks up; eul chooses how it looks.

The stories below are mined from the issue tracker and drive every personalization decision. Each answer lives in one of exactly three mechanisms — point-of-use (interact with the thing itself), pinning (Menu Bar settings), or toggles (Health settings). No fourth mechanism is permitted.
